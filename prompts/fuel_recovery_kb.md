# Fuel and recovery knowledge base

Purpose: a fixed set of entries that turn what a personal training or movement review found (late-session fade, soreness, heavy legs, bad sleep after games, pain notes) into an explanation, a concrete action sized for the player, a check that tells them whether it works, and the source behind it. It goes deeper than the skill KB's recovery entry, which points here.

Audience: an adult recreational basketball player who plays about one game a week and adds a few training sessions (strength, court movement, skill, conditioning). Amounts are per kg of body mass so they fit any player.

How agents use it:
- The movement and training report writers cite entries by slug (`kb: ["FR-game-day"]`) and use an entry only when the session data, the player's notes or their log show the **Situation** (for example a "legs gone in the 4th" note, next-morning pain scores, a sleep log, a weigh-in). If the situation is not in the data, do not use the entry.
- Scale the per-kg numbers in **What to do** with the player's logged body mass (player KB) and say so. Without a logged body mass, give the per-kg values only. Re-scale when the logged mass changes by more than about 3 % (our rule).
- **Check** is what goes into the plan or log so the next review can see whether it worked.
- **Never give medical advice beyond red-flag referral.** No diagnosis of any pain; pain patterns are "possible", "likely" or "unclear". Red flags send the player to a sports physiotherapist or doctor (FR-soreness-vs-pain). Before a big change to diet or supplements, advise an accredited sports dietitian.
- No weight cut while new sprint / plyo load is being built (FR-body-comp). Do not write a deficit into a plan in that period.

Limits:
- Energy numbers come from a prediction equation (individual error likely +/-10-15 %); food values are approximate (national food composition tables, labels vary). The real check is the weekly weight trend plus training quality.
- Under-fuelling and dehydration are plausible contributors to a late-game fade, not established causes (unverified).
- Labels: **Evidence level** is Strong (position stand, consensus or several RCTs/meta-analyses agree), Moderate (some RCTs or a meta-analysis with caveats), Weak (small single studies, mechanism, expert opinion). "(our rule)" marks our own practical choice built from the evidence but not tested as such; present it to the player as our choice, not "the science says". "unverified" marks figures that were not re-checked against a primary source.
- National bodies: the AIS supplement framework, Sports Dietitians Australia (SDA), Sport Integrity Australia (SIA) and the Australian Food Composition Database (AFCD) are cited because they publish clear, free guidance. Every player can use their own country's sports dietitian body, anti-doping agency and food database; the numbers do not change.

Main sources (short names used below):
- **Joint Stand 2016**: ACSM / Academy of Nutrition and Dietetics / Dietitians of Canada, *Nutrition and athletic performance*, Thomas, Erdman & Burke 2016, J Acad Nutr Diet 116(3):501-528, DOI 10.1016/j.jand.2015.12.006.
- **IOC 2018**: Maughan et al., IOC consensus on dietary supplements, Br J Sports Med 52(7):439-455, DOI 10.1136/bjsports-2018-099027.
- **AIS**: AIS Sports Supplement Framework, Group A, https://www.ausport.gov.au/ais/nutrition/supplements/group_a
- **ISSN**: nutrient timing (Kerksick 2017, DOI 10.1186/s12970-017-0189-4), creatine (Kreider 2017, DOI 10.1186/s12970-017-0173-z), caffeine (Guest 2021, DOI 10.1186/s12970-020-00383-4).
- **SDA**: *Food for your sport: basketball*, https://www.sportsdietitians.com.au/factsheets/food-for-your-sport/food-for-your-sport-basketball
- **ACSM 2007**: Sawka et al., fluid replacement position stand, Med Sci Sports Exerc 39(2):377-390, PubMed 17277604.

## Entries

### FR-energy-by-day

**Situation:** Late-session or late-game fade, "feels heavy", flat sessions, constant hunger or weekly weight drifting; or any plan that needs a daily intake target.

**Why:** Needs differ a lot between a desk day and a game day. Basketball is stop-start and uses muscle glycogen heavily; the Joint Stand 2016 says to "fuel for the work required" and to move carbohydrate so availability is high for key sessions. Chronically low energy availability impairs hormones, bone, immunity and training adaptation, in men and women (RED-S).

**What to do (per kg of body mass):**

| Day type | Carbs g/kg | Protein g/kg | Fat |
|---|---|---|---|
| Rest day | 3-3.5 | ~1.6 | 20-35 % of energy |
| Strength or power day | 4-5 | ~1.6 | 20-35 % of energy |
| Court / skill day; day before the game | ~5 | ~1.6 | 20-35 % of energy |
| Game day | 5-6 | ~1.6 | 20-35 % of energy |

Energy, as a starting point:
- **Base:** Mifflin-St Jeor resting rate x 1.4-1.5 for a mostly seated job (higher for an active job).
- **Plus the session:** hard basketball about 8-10 kcal per kg per hour (less with bench time); a strength session about 3-5 kcal per kg net (our estimate).
- **Split:** the g/kg values inside the Joint Stand bands are our rule.
- **If intake must drop:** cut oil, fried food and sweet drinks first, never protein or session carbs (our rule).

**Check:** 3-4 morning weigh-ins a week, compare weekly averages: steady weight plus good session quality = about right (our rule). Watch for low-energy signs: falling performance, constant fatigue, poor sleep, low libido, frequent illness, injuries that will not settle.

**Source:** Joint Stand 2016 (carb bands 3-5 g/kg low/skill, 5-7 g/kg ~1 h/day moderate; fat 20-35 %; low energy availability); Burke et al. 2011, J Sports Sci 29 Suppl 1:S17-S27, DOI 10.1080/02640414.2011.585473; Mifflin et al. 1990, DOI 10.1093/ajcn/51.2.241; basketball cost https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3154898/table/pone-0021822-t001

**Evidence level:** Strong for the bands and the principle; Moderate for the energy estimates; the per-day split is our rule.

### FR-protein

**Situation:** Strength and plyo load is rising, soreness lingers, or the food log shows protein mainly at dinner.

**Why:** Protein added to resistance training raises strength and fat-free mass, with no further fat-free mass gain above about 1.6 g/kg/day. Spreading it over the day keeps muscle protein synthesis topped up; total daily protein matters more than exact timing.

**What to do:**
- **Daily:** ~1.6 g/kg/day as **4 feeds of ~0.4 g/kg** (breakfast, lunch, after training / dinner, pre-bed).
- **Pre-sleep option:** a milk drink or yoghurt (casein-rich, 30-40 g protein in the ISSN wording) on training nights or when dinner was early.
- **Plant protein works:** with tofu or soy milk use a slightly larger serve, or pair it with egg, dairy or meat.
- **Examples:** 3 eggs + 250 mL milk ~27 g; 150 g cooked chicken ~45 g; 200 g firm tofu ~25-30 g; 400 mL soy milk ~13-15 g.

Cue: "A palm and a half of protein, four times a day." / 中文："每天四次，每次一个半手掌大小的蛋白质。"

**Check:** Food log for 3 days: 4 feeds of about 0.3-0.5 g/kg each (our rule). Soreness after strength or plyo days settles within the normal DOMS window (FR-soreness-vs-pain).

**Source:** Morton et al. 2018, Br J Sports Med 52:376-384, DOI 10.1136/bjsports-2017-097608 (49 RCTs, plateau ~1.62 g/kg); Joint Stand 2016 (1.2-2.0 g/kg; 0.3 g/kg after key sessions and every 3-5 h); Schoenfeld & Aragon 2018, DOI 10.1186/s12970-018-0215-1 (0.4 g/kg/meal over >= 4 meals); ISSN Kerksick 2017 (20-40 g every 3-4 h, pre-sleep protein, plant leucine).

**Evidence level:** Strong (daily target); Moderate (per-meal split, pre-sleep).

### FR-carbs-timing

**Situation:** Evening sessions after a desk day; an evening session fuelled by lunch alone; fade in the last third of a session; gut trouble in sessions.

**Why:** Glycogen at the start of a stop-start session matters more than what is taken during it. Fat, fibre and protein close to the start slow digestion and can upset the gut.

**What to do:**
- **Main meal 1-4 h before** sessions over 60 min: 1-4 g/kg carbs (in practice about 1-1.5 g/kg 3-4 h before), lower fat and fibre, moderate protein.
- **Snack 1-2 h before:** carbohydrate-based, low-fat, ~0.5-1 g/kg carbs (a bread roll, a banana + milk, toast with honey).
- **0-30 min before, only if hungry:** half a banana, a few lollies, sips of sports drink (our rule).
- **Fluid** 5-10 mL/kg in the 2-4 h before, pale-yellow urine.
- **After:** a normal carb-containing meal within a couple of hours plus ~0.3 g/kg protein within ~2 h.
- **Speedy refuelling** (1.0-1.2 g/kg/h for 4 h) only when the next demanding session is < 8 h away, which is rare in a rec week.
- Never train an evening session on lunch alone: add a late-afternoon snack (our rule).

**Check:** Session RPE and the last-third numbers in the review (shooting `thirds`, rep rate) on fuelled vs unfuelled days; no gut complaints (our rule).

**Source:** Joint Stand 2016 (1-4 g/kg 1-4 h before; 5-10 mL/kg fluid; 0.3 g/kg protein after; speedy refuelling < 8 h); SDA basketball (low-fat carb snack 1-2 h before); ISSN Kerksick 2017 (wider "anabolic window").

**Evidence level:** Strong for the guideline numbers; the food choices and the "no lunch-only evening session" rule are our rule.

### FR-game-day

**Situation:** Any game day, especially an evening game after work; notes like "legs gone late", "no energy in the 2nd half", or bad sleep after games.

**Why:** Starting the game with full glycogen and fluid matters most for a 40-min rec game. Carbs during play help most when the player starts low on fuel; a carb-electrolyte drink did not beat water in well-hydrated players. Caffeine helps sprints and jumps but costs sleep at evening games.

**What to do (evening game, 5-6 g/kg carbs for the day):**

| Time | What |
|---|---|
| Breakfast | Oats or cereal with milk, eggs, fruit |
| Lunch, ~6-7 h before (main fuelling meal) | A big serve of rice, pasta or potatoes (~1.5 g/kg carbs), lean protein (~0.4 g/kg), cooked vegetables, little oil |
| Mid-afternoon | A bread roll or fruit bread + a glass of milk |
| 1.5-2.5 h before | Banana + crackers or white bread with honey/jam (~0.5-1 g/kg carbs); 400-600 mL water; nothing greasy, very spicy, high-fibre or bulky within ~3 h |
| Warm-up and game | Water; 300-500 mL sports drink (~20-30 g carbs) sipped at time-outs and breaks if the pre-game meal was light or the player usually fades |
| Half-time | Sips of water or the sports drink above; half a banana or a few lollies only if hungry (our rule) |
| Within ~2 h after | Recovery meal: carbs + lean protein (~0.3-0.4 g/kg) + vegetables + water (1.25-1.5 L per kg lost, FR-hydration). If there are drinks after: eat first, alternate with water, keep alcohol light |
| Bedtime | Wind-down routine (FR-sleep); no caffeine after the game; milk/yoghurt only if the post-game meal was small (our rule) |

Caffeine: see FR-supplements (for an evening game, skip or use a low dose).

Cue: "Big carb lunch, snack two hours out, sip, refuel within two hours." / 中文："午饭多吃主食，赛前两小时加餐，比赛小口喝，赛后两小时内吃恢复餐。"

**Check:** Log "legs in 4th quarter" 1-5 and game weigh-in loss; compare games fuelled with the timeline against ones that were not (our rule).

**Source:** Joint Stand 2016 (pre-exercise carbs and fluid; small carb amounts or mouth rinse in 45-75 min high-intensity work, 30-60 g/h in 1-2.5 h stop-start sport; alcohol impairs rehydration and recovery); SDA basketball; Baker et al. 2007, PubMed 17596779 (drink vs water no difference when hydrated); timeline and amounts our rule.

**Evidence level:** Strong (principles); Moderate (applicability of in-game carbs to a 40-min rec game); the timeline is our rule.

### FR-hydration

**Situation:** Big weight drop across a game, dark urine before a game, headaches or cramps after play, salty white crust on clothes, or no sweat test done yet.

**Why:** Basketball skill and sprint drills got worse from about 2 % dehydration (shots fewer, sprint/lateral drills slower, larger as dehydration grew). Sweat rate and sweat sodium vary widely, so a personal test beats a generic number. Over-drinking is also a risk (hyponatraemia).

**What to do:**
- **Limit:** lose < 2 % of body mass per game; aim for about 1-1.5 % at most (our rule).
- **Sweat test**, twice (one training, one game): empty bladder, weigh in minimal clothing; play; note fluid drunk; towel dry and weigh again.
  - Sweat loss (L) = (pre - post kg) + fluid drunk (L) - urine (L)
  - Sweat rate = loss / hours
  - % change = (pre - post) / pre x 100
- **After:** replace **1.25-1.5 L per kg lost** over the next hours, with sodium from food or drink; do not restrict salt after big sweat losses. A salted meal or a broth usually covers the sodium.
- **Sodium during play** only if sweat rate > 1.2 L/h, sweat is salty, or play lasts > 2 h; electrolyte tablets only if the player's test shows it (our rule).
- Drink to the plan plus thirst; do not force fluid.

**Check:** Game loss under the limit on the scale; morning and pre-game urine pale straw.

**Source:** ACSM 2007 (prevent > 2 % loss; individual testing; urine colour); Baker, Dougherty, Chow & Kenney 2007, Med Sci Sports Exerc 39(7):1114-1123, PubMed 17596779 (17 players, decline significant from 2 %; caveat: dehydration induced by 3 h walking in heat); Joint Stand 2016 (1.25-1.5 L/kg, sodium > 1.2 L/h, ~1 g sodium per L sweat on average, over-drinking risk). Typical indoor sweat rates (0.8-1.5 L/h) are unverified; the player's own test replaces them.

**Evidence level:** Strong (targets and replacement); Moderate (basketball performance effect).

**Stop and get help:** headache, dizziness, nausea or confusion during or after play (possible heat illness or hyponatraemia).

### FR-supplements

**Situation:** The player asks about supplements, or the plan considers one. Only AIS Group A items are discussed.

**Why:** Supplements add only a little on top of a good diet, carry a contamination risk, and should be trialled in training first. AIS Group A means **strong evidence for use in specific situations in sport with evidence-based protocols, not "good for everyone"**. Group A performance supplements: caffeine, beta-alanine, dietary nitrate, sodium bicarbonate, creatine, glycerol.

**What to do:**
- **Creatine monohydrate: 3 g/day, no loading**, with any meal, plain powder (our rule inside the ISSN 3-5 g/day).
  - Fits repeated sprints, jumps and strength work.
  - Trade-off: it commonly adds ~0.5-2 kg, mostly water in muscle (unverified range). For a player worried about body mass, skip the loading phase and watch scale and jump tests.
  - Without loading, muscle saturation takes ~3-4 weeks (standard ISSN figure, unverified in full text).
  - Tell the doctor before a blood test (it can raise creatinine); anyone with kidney disease asks a doctor first.
- **Caffeine: 3 mg/kg about 60 min before**, only when bedtime is more than 6 h away (in practice a game starting before ~6 pm; our rule).
  - Evening game: skip it, or 1-2 mg/kg (about one coffee) and accept a later, lighter sleep; never an energy drink after the game (our rule).
  - Even 6 h is not fully safe: 400 mg taken 6 h before bed cut measured sleep by over 1 h without people noticing (n = 12).
  - Trial in training first.
- **Not worth it now:**
  - beta-alanine (helps 1-4 min efforts; basketball efforts are < 10 s);
  - bicarbonate and glycerol (other sports, gut upset);
  - BCAAs, glutamine, test boosters, fat burners, collagen for performance, most recovery blends, HMB, tart cherry (inconsistent);
  - high-dose vitamin C/E around training (may blunt adaptation; food sources are fine).
  - Beetroot juice: optional, mixed evidence; leafy greens (spinach, rocket, lettuce) are a food source of nitrate.
- **Protein powder** is a Group A sports food: a convenience tool when a meal is far away, not a requirement. Vitamin D, iron etc. only after a blood test shows a deficiency.
- **Buy batch-tested products** (HASTA or Informed Sport) and check the batch with the national anti-doping agency's app; an SIA-commissioned 2022 survey found about 1 in 3 of 200 online supplements contained a prohibited substance.

**Check:** Creatine: weekly weight average and jump/sprint tests over 4 weeks; stop if the player feels heavier and jumps do not improve (our rule). Caffeine: sleep hours logged on game nights.

**Source:** IOC 2018; AIS Group A page; ISSN Kreider 2017 (creatine efficacy and safety; dosing figures unverified in full text); ISSN Guest 2021 (3-6 mg/kg ~60 min before; team-sport sprints and jumps); Drake et al. 2013, J Clin Sleep Med 9(11):1195-1200, PMC3805807; Trexler 2015, DOI 10.1186/s12970-015-0090-y; SIA https://www.sportintegrity.gov.au/supplements and https://www.sportintegrity.gov.au/news/media-statements/2025-04/sport-supplements-survey

**Evidence level:** Strong (creatine and caffeine for performance); Moderate (caffeine sleep cost, beta-alanine fit, antioxidants); the dose choices are our rule.

### FR-sleep

**Situation:** Sleep log under 7 h, trouble falling asleep after evening games, irregular wake time, or flat sessions the day after a game.

**Why:** Sleep is the cheapest and biggest recovery lever. Athletes often sleep < 7 h with fragmented sleep, and competition timing disturbs it. In a small, uncontrolled study, basketball players who extended sleep ran faster sprints and shot better.

**What to do:**
- Target **7-9 h**, wake time consistent (+/- 1 h, our rule).
- Before a game: an extra 30-60 min in bed on the 2 nights before (our rule).
- Late-game wind-down (our rule, from the Walsh 2021 toolbox and Drake 2013):
  - no caffeine within 6 h of bed;
  - after the game: a cool shower, the recovery meal, dim lights, screens down, tomorrow's to-do list written, alcohol low;
  - accept falling asleep later but keep the wake time;
  - a 20-30 min early-afternoon nap the next day if possible.
- Bedroom cool, dark, quiet.

Cue: "Same wake time, even after a late game." / 中文："晚场比赛后起床时间也不变。"

**Check:** Log sleep hours nightly; weekly average >= 7 h, game-night loss made up within 2 days (our rule).

**Source:** Hirshkowitz et al. 2015, Sleep Health 1(1):40-43, DOI 10.1016/j.sleh.2014.12.010 (7-9 h); Walsh et al. 2021, Br J Sports Med 55(7):356-368, DOI 10.1136/bjsports-2020-102025; Mah et al. 2011, Sleep 34(7):943-950, DOI 10.5665/SLEEP.1132 (n = 11); Drake et al. 2013.

**Evidence level:** Strong (health and the athlete consensus); Weak-Moderate (sleep extension for performance).

### FR-recovery-ranked

**Situation:** The player asks what to do for recovery, buys gadgets, takes ice baths after lifting, or stretches to fix soreness.

**Why:** The big levers are sleep, food and sensible load. Passive methods reduce soreness and perceived fatigue a little; massage ranked best for soreness in a large meta-analysis, stretching showed no effect. Cold-water immersion after strength training blunted strength and muscle gains, which are the point of strength and power sessions.

**What to do (ranked, our rule built on the evidence):**

| Rank | Method | Use |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Sleep 7-9 h | Daily (FR-sleep) |
| 2 | Enough energy, carbs, protein, fluid | Daily (FR-energy-by-day, FR-protein, FR-hydration) |
| 3 | Sensible load progression, planned easy days | FR-load-progression |
| 4 | Light active recovery: walk, easy bike or swim 15-30 min + mobility | Day after the game, not a hard run |
| 5 | Massage, incl. self-massage tools | Nice to have; professional once a fortnight if budget allows |
| 6 | Compression (calf sleeves, tights) | Optional, a few hours after a game |
| 7 | Cold-water immersion | After games only, 10-15 min at ~10-15 °C if it helps; **not within ~4-6 h after a strength or power session** while building strength |
| 8 | Foam rolling | 30-60 s per muscle in the warm-up, not as a cure |
| 9 | Sauna | If enjoyed; not counted as recovery; rehydrate |
| 10 | Static stretching | For range of motion, not for soreness |

A cold shower for comfort is fine any time.

**Check:** Recovery effort is spent in rank order: sleep and food logged before any gadget (our rule).

**Source:** Dupuy et al. 2018, Front Physiol 9:403, DOI 10.3389/fphys.2018.00403 (99 studies; massage best for DOMS and fatigue; stretching no effect); Roberts et al. 2015, J Physiol 593(18):4285-4301, DOI 10.1113/JP270570; Grgic 2022, PubMed 35068365 (ES -0.23 on strength); Piñero et al. 2024, Eur J Sport Sci 24(2):177-189; Hill et al. 2014, Br J Sports Med 48(18):1340-1346, DOI 10.1136/bjsports-2013-092456 (compression); Wiewelhove et al. 2019, Front Physiol 10:376, DOI 10.3389/fphys.2019.00376 (foam rolling); Herbert et al. 2011 Cochrane, DOI 10.1002/14651858.CD004577.pub3 (stretching; unverified); sauna review https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41032138/ (unclear).

**Evidence level:** Moderate (most methods; many small studies); Weak/unclear (sauna); the ranking itself is our rule.

### FR-soreness-vs-pain

**Situation:** Muscle soreness after running, sprints or jumps, joint aches after games, or any pain note in a session log.

**Why:** Delayed-onset muscle soreness (DOMS) after new or eccentric work is normal; pain that is sharp, local, one-sided or rising needs a professional. In Achilles tendinopathy patients, continued running/jumping with pain kept at or below ~5/10 during activity and settled by the next morning gave results similar to rest. This KB does not diagnose.

**What to do:**

| Likely DOMS (normal) | Possible injury: get it checked if it persists |
|---|---|
| Starts 12-24 h after, peaks 24-72 h, gone by ~5 days | Sharp pain during activity, or a pop/snap |
| Diffuse ache over the muscle belly, both legs similar | One clear spot, one side, tender on a tendon or bone |
| Eases with the warm-up | Gets worse with more activity |
| No or mild swelling | Swelling, bruising, warmth, limping |
| Follows new or harder work | Returns after every session or does not settle in a week |

- **Pain score rule (our rule):** score each sore area (e.g. calf, Achilles, knee) 0-10 the night after and the next morning.
  - Next morning <= 3/10 and back to baseline in 24-48 h: keep training and building strength.
  - Rising week to week: cut jumping/running volume and see a physio.
  - During activity, stay at or below ~5/10 (Silbernagel model, with a physio).
- **Red flags -> sports physiotherapist or doctor:** sharp or clearly localised pain; swelling; pain at night or at rest that does not ease; limping or cannot bear weight; pain rising during a session; knee locks, catches or gives way; cannot fully straighten the knee; hot, red joint or fever; numbness or tingling; pain worsening over 2-3 weeks despite less load.
- **Same-day doctor / emergency:** a pop or "kicked in the back of the leg" feeling with inability to push up on the toes; a calf that is swollen, warm, red or tender at rest, especially after a flight or long sitting, or with breathlessness or chest pain (possible blood clot).
- Capacity work (heel raises, isometrics) belongs to `movement_kb.md` / the program; this entry only gates it.

**Check:** Morning pain scores in the log; trend flat or falling across weeks.

**Source:** DOMS table and red flags: general clinical teaching (our rule); Silbernagel et al. 2007, Am J Sports Med 35(6):897-906, DOI 10.1177/0363546506298279; Collins et al. 2018, Br J Sports Med 52(18):1170-1178, DOI 10.1136/bjsports-2018-099397 (exercise therapy for patellofemoral pain, context).

**Evidence level:** Moderate (pain-monitoring model); the 3/10 next-morning threshold is our rule.

### FR-load-progression

**Situation:** New sprint, plyo or running volume is being added; soreness or pain scores climb; 2-3 warning signs persist; a planned deload week.

**Why:** Big sudden spikes in one type of load (sprint metres, jump contacts, running minutes) on tissue that is not yet adapted are the practical risk. The 10 % rule did not prevent injuries in a novice-runner RCT, and the acute:chronic workload ratio is not a reliable injury predictor, so the rule here is simple tracking and small steps.

**What to do (our rule unless cited):**
- **Log** each session in one line: date, type, minutes, session RPE (0-10), sprint metres or jump contacts, next-morning pain scores (0-10), sleep hours.
- **Increase one load type at a time, ~10-20 % per week**; hold or drop it when next-morning pain goes above 3/10.
- **Plyos start low:** ~40-60 foot contacts per session; no full plyo program on top of the game week in week 1.
- **Spacing:** keep >= 48 h between the two highest-impact sessions of the week (the game and the hardest jump/sprint session) where possible; no hardest jump/sprint session the day after a game in the first 4 weeks.
- **Deload** every 4th week: cut volume ~40-50 % (fewer sets, jumps, sprints), keep intensity moderate-high, keep the game.
- **Deload early** if 2-3 of these last more than a few days: next-morning pain > 3/10, resting heart rate clearly up, worse sleep, low motivation, jump or sprint tests clearly worse.

**Check:** Weekly log shows only one load type rising; pain scores stay <= 3/10; jump/sprint tests hold or improve after each deload.

**Source:** Buist et al. 2008, Am J Sports Med 36(1):33-39, DOI 10.1177/0363546507307505 (20.8 % vs 20.3 % injuries); ACWR critique: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8138569/ and Impellizzeri et al. 2020, https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7534938/ ; deload: common strength and conditioning practice.

**Evidence level:** Moderate (what does not work); Weak-Moderate (deload practice); thresholds are our rule.

### FR-body-comp

**Situation:** The player says they feel heavy or wants to lose fat; a plan is about to include a deficit; weight is falling while new load is being built.

**Why:** Sprint acceleration and jumping depend on force relative to body mass, so losing fat helps only if strength holds; losing muscle lowers power. A deficit while tissues adapt to new sprint/plyo load costs recovery and adaptation. "Heavy" legs and late fade can also come from low fuel, poor sleep, low fitness or sore legs; fix those first. BMI says little about body fat in someone who lifts.

**What to do:**
- **While building new sprint / plyo load: no cut. Eat at maintenance.** A very slow drift (about -0.3 % of body mass per week, small deficit on rest days only) is the most to allow, and only if the player chooses it (our rule).
- **After that block, if they want it:**
  - slow loss, <= ~0.7 % of body mass per week;
  - ~300-500 kcal/day below maintenance (our estimate);
  - protein 1.8-2.2 g/kg;
  - carbs kept around sessions; the deficit comes from oil, fried food, alcohol and sweet drinks on rest days.
- **Judge by** weekly average weight, waist measurement, photos, and jump/sprint tests, not BMI.

**Check:** Weekly weight average within ~0.5 % of the start while building load (our rule); jump/sprint tests not worse.

**Source:** Joint Stand 2016 (plan weight loss away from key competition; < 1 %/week preserves fat-free mass and performance; higher protein in a deficit); Garthe et al. 2011, Int J Sport Nutr Exerc Metab 21(2):97-104, DOI 10.1123/ijsnem.21.2.97 (0.7 %/week gained lean mass and strength, 1.4 %/week did not; small study); Morton et al. 2018.

**Evidence level:** Strong (slow-loss principle); Moderate (Garthe rates); the no-cut-while-building choice is our rule.

## Plate method (our rule)

Adapted from the US Olympic & Paralympic "athlete plates" idea and the Joint Stand periodisation. Works with any cuisine: picture a dinner plate, or the shared dishes plus your own bowl.

| Day type | Carbs (rice, pasta, bread, potato, noodles) | Protein (meat, fish, tofu, egg, beans) | Vegetables | Oil |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Rest day | 1/4 plate | 1/4 plate (1-1.5 palms) | 1/2 plate | small |
| Training day | 1/3 plate | 1/3 plate | 1/3 plate | moderate |
| Game day / day before | 1/2 plate | 1/4 plate | 1/4 plate, cooked (no big raw salads before the game) | low before the game |

Hand portions per meal:
- protein 1-1.5 palms

### Pre-game snacks (1-2 h before, ~0.5-1 g/kg carbs, low fat)

1. A bread roll, bagel or a few slices of white bread with honey or jam.
2. Banana + a glass of milk or soy milk (sweetened soy milk adds ~10-15 g carbs).
3. Rice crackers + sports drink.

### Post-game options (~0.3-0.4 g/kg protein + carbs + fluid)

1. A rice or noodle bowl with chicken, beef or tofu and vegetables.
2. A noodle soup with lean meat; the broth helps with sodium.
3. Quick option: 500 mL flavoured milk (~17 g protein, ~24 g carbs plus added sugar) + a bread roll or sandwich.

## One-week recovery template (our rule)

An example week with **one evening game on Thursday** and four training sessions. Move the days with the game; keep these rules:
- no hard jump / sprint session the day after a game;
- the day before a game is light (short skill work);
- >= 48 h between the game and the hardest jump / sprint session.

| Day | Session | Fuel (FR-energy-by-day) | Recovery |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mon | Strength (lower body, braking) | 4-5 g/kg carbs; ~0.3 g/kg protein after | No ice bath within 4-6 h; pre-bed milk; morning pain check Tue |
| Tue | Court movement: speed, deceleration (~40-60 foot contacts early on) | 4-5 g/kg; snack 1-2 h before | Easy evening walk optional |
| Wed | Skill: short, crisp, low volume | ~5 g/kg (top up) | Early night; sleep banking (+30-60 min) |
| Thu | **Game** (evening) | 5-6 g/kg, FR-game-day timeline; sweat test | Post-game meal + fluids; cold-water immersion optional; wind-down; same wake time Fri |
| Fri | Active recovery: walk, swim or easy bike 15-30 min + mobility | 3-3.5 g/kg; rehydrate | Compression optional; nap if possible; pain score morning |
| Sat | Power + conditioning (jumps, sprints, med ball, short intervals) | 4-5 g/kg | No ice bath within 4-6 h; pain score Sun morning |
| Sun | Rest | 3-3.5 g/kg; protein steady | Extra sleep; massage if any |

Deload every 4th week: same days, volume cut ~40-50 % (FR-load-progression).

Daily constants:
- creatine 3 g if the player uses it
- 4 feeds of ~0.4 g/kg protein
- 7-9 h sleep
- weekly weight average
