# Strength and conditioning for basketball

Source keys resolve in `../library/SOURCES.md`. `(coach rule of thumb)` = common practice, not tested by a cited study.

## Does strength transfer to the court?

- Stronger athletes tend to jump higher, sprint faster and change direction better. [suchomel2016]
- More strength is also linked to lower injury risk. [suchomel2016]
- Squat strength gains correlate with sprint gains (r = -0.77); mean sprint improvement about 3%. [seitz2014]
- Kettlebell swings and jump squats both raised squat strength and jump height in 6 weeks. [lake2012]
- Weaker players: build strength first. Stronger players: add more power work. [suchomel2018]
- Catch-free Olympic pulls train explosive triple extension with less technique to learn. [suchomel2015]

## Deceleration (stops, cuts, landings)

- Braking has very high impact forces in the first ~50 ms of contact. [harper2022]
- Two qualities matter: controlling braking force and absorbing it. [harper2022]
- Eccentric, isometric, concentric and reactive strength all contribute, plus braking technique. [harper2022]
- Library picks: `drop_landing`, `walking_lunge`, `rdl`, `nordic`, `lateral_bound`, `step_down`.

## Speed and the ankle

- Soleus and gastrocnemius supply most of the support force when running up to about 7 m/s. [dorn2012]
- So train both: straight-knee and bent-knee calf raises, plus `pogo`. (coach rule of thumb, based on dorn2012)
- Contact time and RSI help pick and monitor fast plyometrics. [flanagan2008]

## Injury-risk programmes with trial evidence

- Nordic hamstring: 25 sessions in 13 weeks cut hamstring injuries in amateur soccer. [vanderhorst2015]
- Copenhagen adduction: 3x/week preseason, 1x/week in season, cut groin problems in football. [haroy2019]
- Both trials were in football (soccer), not basketball. Transfer is likely but not tested here.

## In-season week

**Principles**

- 1-2 strength sessions a week is common in season; direct basketball trials on frequency are lacking. (coach rule of thumb)
- Keep intensity, cut volume: 2-3 hard sets per main lift at RIR 2-3. (coach rule of thumb)
- Heavy lower body at least 48 h before a game. (coach rule of thumb)
- Microdosing (10-20 min bouts) fits around practice; evidence is mostly indirect. [cuthbert2024]
- Velocity loss around 20% keeps fatigue low and favoured jump gains. [pareja2017]

**Example: game Wednesday, long pickup Sunday** (coach rule of thumb)

| Day | Focus | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Mon | Main strength (legs) | trap bar or front squat 3x3, BSS 2x6, Nordic 2x4 |
| Tue | Light power, primer | box jump 3x3, pogo 2x10, med ball 3x3 |
| Wed | Game | warm-up: glute bridge, Spanish squat, pogo |
| Thu | Recovery, upper body | push-up, row, Pallof, Copenhagen, bike zone 2 |
| Fri | Second strength (legs) | hang clean pull 3x3, hip thrust 3x6, calf raises |
| Sat | Off or microdose | 10 min: goblet squat 2x5, side plank |
| Sun | Long pickup | warm-up only; no heavy lifting the day before or after |

- Swap Monday and Friday loads if Sunday pickup left you sore. (coach rule of thumb)
- Two games in a week: drop Friday to a microdose. (coach rule of thumb)

## Joint-friendly variants

**Knee (patellar tendon) pain**

- Isometrics (Spanish squat, wall sit) reduced tendon pain in a very small study; try them, do not promise. [rio2015]
- Progressive loading (isometric, then slow strength, then jumps, then sport) beat eccentric-only rehab. [breda2021]
- Front squat loads the knee less than back squat at the loads tested. [gullett2009]
- Use a box to limit squat depth; prefer reverse over forward lunges. (coach rule of thumb)
- Pain up to about 3/10 that settles by the next day is a common working limit. (coach rule of thumb)

**Achilles / calf**

- Heavy slow calf raises worked as well as eccentric training for Achilles tendinopathy. [beyer2015]
- Use bent- and straight-knee raises; add pogo only when they are pain-free. (coach rule of thumb)

**Low back**

- Trap bar over straight bar; front or goblet squat over back squat. [swinton2011] [gullett2009]
- BSS works the legs with less spinal load than back squats. [mackey2021]
- Core work that resists movement (Pallof, side plank, dead bug, carries). (coach rule of thumb)

## Long-term view

- Develop strength and movement skill over years, not one season. [nsca_ltad2016]
- Adult recreational players respond to the same principles; start lighter and progress steadily. [acsm2009]
